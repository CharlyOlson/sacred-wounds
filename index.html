About This Work

This is not an attack on faith.
It is an autopsy of language.

The phrases collected here were spoken inside sanctuaries, written in letters, delivered from pulpits. They were framed as grace. Many caused measurable, lasting harm to the abused, the questioning, the grieving, the different, the poor.

This piece does not argue that God does not exist. It argues that words wielded as weapons inside spaces of trust carry a specific and serious weight — and that naming them clearly is the first act of repair.

“The most dangerous phrases are the ones that sound like love.”
