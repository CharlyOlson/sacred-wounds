The Project

What is Sacred Wounds?

"Sacred Wounds" is an art project about the words that were supposed to heal—but didn't.

It focuses on the language often used in religious spaces: the phrases people hear when they're grieving, questioning, coming into themselves, or just trying to belong. These are the lines repeated from pulpits, small groups, and quiet conversations—phrases that sound comforting on the surface, but often carry something heavier underneath.

Each piece takes one of those phrases and breaks it open. What was actually said. What it implied. What it did to the person hearing it. And then, what should have been said instead.

Because for a lot of people, the harm wasn't always loud or obvious. It didn't always look like rejection. Sometimes it sounded like guidance. Sometimes it came wrapped in care. But over time, those words taught people to shrink, to doubt themselves, to disconnect from their own experiences in order to stay "acceptable."

This project exists for the people who were told their questions were dangerous. That their identity needed fixing. That their pain was a test, a punishment, or something to pray away.

It doesn't exist to tear down belief or faith. It exists to draw a line between belief and harm—to show what happens when language is used to control instead of support, to silence instead of listen.

"Sacred Wounds" is about naming those moments clearly, without dressing them up. It's about giving people the words they didn't have at the time—and maybe, finally, the ones they needed to hear.

Meet the Artist

Ansel James

🎭

Ansel James

Savant • Redblooded American • Gay • Artist

Ansel is a Kansas City-based artist with a thespian and artistic nature that flows through everything he does—creating isn't just what he does, it's how he breathes.

Raised on Christianity, Ansel's journey through faith, identity, and healing deeply informs the work of Sacred Wounds. His lived experience of navigating questions, doubt, and the gap between what was preached and what was practiced fuels the honesty and rawness of this project.

With a fantastic mustache and the kind of humble confidence that makes him approachable and real, Ansel creates work that matters—art that says the things people have been too afraid or too broken to say themselves.
